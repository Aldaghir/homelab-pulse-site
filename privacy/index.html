Privacy Policy

Effective 14 November 2026

In short

  • No accounts, no ads, no tracking, and no analytics libraries.
  • Your server's data travels only between your phone and your own server.
  • From the app, only two things ever reach us: alerts that are end-to-end encrypted so we cannot read them (when your phone gets push notifications), and crash reports (if you turn them on). If you buy Pro, Google Play also shows us the order, never your payment details.
  • Google's own libraries in the app, for push notifications, the code scanner and Google Play billing, send Google details of the phone and of how they work (sections 4, 6 and 15).
  • Google Play's Data safety answers are listed one by one in section 18.

1. Who we are

Homelab Pulse (“the app”) is developed by Aldaghir (“we”, “us”). It works together with the Homelab Pulse agent, a program you install on your own server. You can reach us at android@aldaghir.com.

2. Data between your phone and your server

The app talks directly to the agent on your server to show system metrics, storage and network figures, services and containers, logs, alerts, terminal sessions and, with Pro, the files you browse and download, and to apply the settings you choose. This data:

  • travels directly between your phone and your server: on your local network over TLS, with your server's certificate pinned from the pairing code, or remotely through your own Cloudflare Tunnel and Cloudflare Access if you set them up (Cloudflare then handles that traffic under your Cloudflare account and Cloudflare's privacy policy);
  • is never sent to us.

3. Data stored on your phone

  • The servers you add: name, address, certificate fingerprint and the agent's features.
  • Access tokens for your servers, the end of your access if it is a temporary guest access, Cloudflare Access credentials if you use them, the push token from Google with a copy of the token each server was given (while you have a server, section 4), and the private key that decrypts your push notifications, encrypted with a key kept by the Android Keystore. And for each server, a key that confirms sensitive actions after your fingerprint or screen lock, which the Android Keystore keeps and never lets out.
  • If you remove a server while it cannot be reached, what the app needs to tell it later that this phone is unpaired, and nothing else: its address at home and certificate fingerprint, this phone's device identifier and access token there, and, if you use them, its tunnel address and Cloudflare Access credentials. They are encrypted like the other secrets, in a file of their own that no exported backup holds or changes. Android's task scheduler keeps the server's identifier and when to try next. The app tries in the background whenever the phone has a network, waiting longer each time, and deletes what it kept once the server confirms the unpairing, or after 7 days at the latest, told or not. The task scheduler then keeps a short record of these tries, the server's identifier among it, until it clears it by itself a day or more later.
  • The latest data the app showed you from your servers, so it can show it with its age when there is no connection: readings, server details, storage (disks, their mount points and health, Docker images), containers and services, alerts and their rules, the state of backups, updates, security, network and Uptime Kuma, checks (without the credentials in their targets), the timeline (including SSH sign-ins with their user names and addresses), devices and the audit log (including the address each action came from). It stays until newer data replaces it, you remove or re-pair the server, your temporary access ends, or the server refuses this phone. The app does not encrypt this copy; it stays in the app's private storage (see the security page).
  • While the home-screen widget is on your home screen, a short note of what it last showed for the selected server: its name, whether all is well, how many alerts are active and how many critical, whether it could be reached, and its processor, memory and temperature rounded to 5 points, so that the widget is redrawn only when this changes. Each refresh replaces it: every 30 minutes while the phone has a network, and after each alert pushed to your phone. It is deleted when you remove the last widget or a server, and none is kept without a widget. The Quick Settings tile keeps no note: it shows the latest data above. With the app lock on, the widget and the tile still show this summary, and the tile may show on the lock screen.
  • On a server where you are a viewer, which alerts you removed from your list (their identifiers and when you removed them), so that they stay off this phone and its notifications. Nothing goes to the server, and they go when the server no longer lists those alerts, you remove or re-pair the server, your temporary access ends, or the server refuses this phone.
  • With Pro: the files you download, in the folder you choose; they are yours and stay there. While a download is running or paused, the app keeps what it needs to go on: the server, the paths and names of the files and folders and the files' sizes, the folder you chose, a short-lived ticket from your server, and how far it got (the files and folders made so far, how much of each file is saved, why it paused, and the version tag your server gave each file, made from its size, its place on the disk and when it changed). This goes when the download ends, you cancel it, or you remove the server, and the app then normally gives up its permission to read and write in the folder you chose, unless another download still uses it. For one file, that permission is to the file itself and stays once the file is downloaded, so that its notification can open it, until Android drops the oldest ones past its limit. If you choose where to save a file while the app is not yet connected to your server (Android may hold the network back from the app while you choose), the download waits for the connection before it starts: meanwhile the app keeps the place and name of the file you chose, with Android's task scheduler too, so that this file, still empty, is deleted if the download never starts (you cancel it, leave the file's screen, or the app is closed first); the scheduler keeps that short record until it clears it by itself, a day or more later. Android's task scheduler keeps a short record of a download that ended or was cancelled until it clears it by itself, a day or more later, even if you remove the server: for one file, the server, the file's path, name, size and version tag, the folder, why it failed if it did, and the ticket, which expires within a day; for several files, the server, the download's name (the folder's, or that of the first item you chose), the number of files and their total size, how many were downloaded or failed and how much was downloaded, and when it ended and why. While a download runs, its notification shows its name and progress; the notification that tells how it ended stays until you dismiss it, or until you remove the server while Android still keeps the download's record.
  • If you buy Pro on Google Play: when Google Play last confirmed the purchase, and nothing else about it (section 15).
  • Your settings in the app, including the quick commands you write for the terminal.

This data is excluded from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. If you export a backup, the file holds your servers, their access tokens and other secrets, and your settings, encrypted with a password you choose, and stays wherever you save it; it holds no copy of the push token, which belongs to this phone, and restoring it registers this phone's own. Removing a server, or uninstalling the app, deletes the related data from your phone, except that removing a server leaves the task scheduler's short record of its downloads, which Android clears by itself a day or more later, and, if the server could not be reached, what is needed to unpair this phone from it, for 7 days at most, and the task scheduler's short record of those tries, which Android clears by itself a day or more later (above); files you downloaded stay in the folder you chose.

4. Push notifications

If your phone has Google Play services and you add a server (the demo server does not count), the app registers with Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM), a Google service, to receive alerts. Registering creates a push token and a Firebase installation identifier at Google, and sends Google details of the phone and the app: the phone's brand, product name and device code name, its form factor (such as watch, TV or car) and Android version, the store that installed the app, the app's version, the oldest Android version it runs on and the one it targets, and the versions of its Firebase libraries and of Kotlin, as Google's Firebase data disclosure describes. It does not happen before you add your first server, and while you have one the app has no setting to turn it off; turning notifications off in Android's settings hides them. The app keeps the push token, and a copy of the token it gave each server, encrypted on your phone only while you have a server; no backup you export holds them, and removing a server deletes its copy. When you remove your last server, the app stops renewing the registration, deletes every copy of the token from your phone, and deletes the push token and the installation identifier at Google once the phone has a network, trying again until Google has deleted them (Android's task scheduler decides when, and keeps only that this is owed, nothing of the token). Firebase's own file on your phone then keeps the deleted identifier, marked as unused, until a new one replaces it or you clear the app's storage. If Google sends a new token while you have no server, the app neither keeps it nor gives it to anyone. Adding a server again registers anew, with a new token and installation identifier. Until they are deleted, Google keeps them under Google's privacy policy.

The app gives the push token to your agent, which keeps it on your server. When an alert fires, your agent encrypts it for your phone (X25519 and AES-256-GCM) and sends the sealed message, with the push token, to Pulse Relay, a small service we run on Cloudflare Workers that hands it to FCM. The relay cannot read your alerts, stores nothing, and does not log tokens, addresses or message contents; to limit abuse, it counts each token's messages per minute by a hash of the token. Cloudflare, as our hosting provider, handles the network connection (including your server's IP address) to deliver each request, under Cloudflare's privacy policy. FCM then delivers the message under Google's privacy policy. You can run your own relay instead, or rely on Telegram alerts from your agent and not use push at all.

5. Crash reports (optional, off by default)

If you turn on “Send crash reports” in Settings, the app keeps a report when it crashes, the five newest at most, and sends it the next time it starts, through your own agent; a report it could not send within 30 days is deleted. A report contains the app and Android versions, the phone's maker and model, when the crash happened, the name of the app's thread that crashed, the types of the errors and where in the code they happened, and the error messages with addresses, host names, e-mail addresses, tokens and home folder names removed.

Your agent writes the report, with this phone's name, to your server's system log and, when a relay is set in its configuration (the configuration it is installed with sets ours), forwards it to us. We use crash reports only to find and fix bugs. They stay in our relay's log on Cloudflare for at most 7 days and are then deleted automatically. To limit abuse, the relay counts reports per minute by a hash of the sending server's address, which it does not log. Nothing is sent while the setting is off, and turning it off deletes any report still waiting on your phone. A crash in the first moment of a start, before the app has read the setting, is written on the phone even with the setting off, and deleted at the next start without being sent.

6. Scanning a pairing code

The app reads pairing QR codes with Google code scanner, part of Google Play services. Google Play services handles the camera, the scan runs on your phone, and the app receives only the text of the code. Google may collect diagnostic data about the scanner's use, such as the device model, app version and performance figures, with identifiers for the installation and the scanning session that are not meant to identify you or your phone, as described in Google's ML Kit data disclosure.

When Google code scanner is missing or cannot open on your phone, the app scans with its own scanner instead. It asks for the camera permission the first time, reads the camera's picture on your phone only while the scanner is open, and keeps or sends no picture: only the text of the code is used. You can type the code instead of scanning it.

7. Services you connect

Integrations you set up, such as Telegram, Cloudflare or Uptime Kuma, are run by your agent. Credentials you enter in the app go directly to your agent, which stores them encrypted on your server; they never reach us. Those services' own terms and privacy policies apply to them.

8. Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to reach your server and choose between your local network and remote access.
  • Wi-Fi multicast: to find agents on your local network.
  • Notifications: to show alerts, and with Pro a download's progress and how it ended; asked for when needed.
  • Camera: only for the app's own QR scanner, when Google code scanner cannot open; asked for the first time. The picture is read on your phone and is not kept or sent.
  • Biometrics: for the optional app lock. Android checks your fingerprint or face; the app never receives biometric data.
  • Run at startup and wake lock: added by Android libraries to deliver notifications and refresh widgets, and with Pro to resume a download after the phone restarts.
  • Foreground service (data sync): with Pro, to keep a download transferring while the app is in the background, its progress shown in a notification.
  • Receive push messages: added by Firebase Cloud Messaging, to receive alerts (section 4).
  • Google Play billing: in the Google Play edition, to buy Pro and confirm it with Google Play (section 15).

9. What we never do

We have no user accounts, show no ads and include no analytics, advertising or tracking libraries. We do not sell or share personal data, and we do not build profiles. Diagnostic data leaves the app in only two ways: the crash reports you turn on, which we use to fix bugs (section 5), and what Google's own libraries send Google about the phone and their work, for push notifications, the code scanner and Google Play billing (sections 4, 6 and 15).

10. Children

The app is a tool for managing servers and is not directed at children under 13, or under the minimum age in your country.

11. Security

Every connection is encrypted with TLS. On your home network, your phone accepts only your server's own certificate, whose fingerprint it got when pairing; away from home, it connects over HTTPS through Cloudflare Access. Secrets on the phone are encrypted with Android Keystore keys. The agent stores only hashes of device tokens and enforces a permission level for each device. The copy the app keeps for offline use is not encrypted by the app, and the home-screen widget and the Quick Settings tile show a summary of it without the app lock (section 3). No system is perfectly secure; if you find a vulnerability, please tell us at android@aldaghir.com.

12. Your choices

  • Turn crash reports on or off at any time in Settings.
  • Turn notifications off in Android settings. Removing a server unpairs your phone from it, so it stops sending you alerts. If the server cannot be reached then, the app says so and unpairs your phone once it can, within 7 days; alerts the server sends meanwhile are dropped on your phone, never shown.
  • We keep no account or profile about you. The only data we hold are crash reports, which are deleted within 7 days, the emails you send us, and, if you buy Pro, what Google Play shows us about your order (section 15). You can write to us with any request (section 17).

15. Buying Pro on Google Play

Pro is sold in the Google Play edition only, as a one-time purchase through Google Play's billing system.

  • What Google handles: the payment and your payment details, under Google Play's terms and Google's privacy policy. The app never sees them.
  • What reaches the app: on your phone, Google Play tells the app which of its products your Google account owns, whether a payment is still pending, and a purchase token, which the app sends back to Google Play once to acknowledge a new purchase. The price you see comes from Google Play too.
  • What Google's billing library sends: the app must use Google's billing library to sell through Google Play. When the app connects to Google Play through it to ask about Pro (when the app starts and on the Pro screen, whether or not you buy) or to buy, the library also sends Google its own logs about its work, such as the results of its calls, over HTTPS. We do not receive these logs, and we found no page where Google says what they hold; Google keeps them under its privacy policy.
  • What the app keeps: only when Google Play last confirmed Pro, sealed on your phone with a key kept by the Android Keystore where the phone's Keystore works, so that Pro keeps working for up to 30 days while Google Play cannot be reached. It is not in the backups you export.
  • What we see: the app sends us nothing about your purchase, and no server of ours checks it; the app trusts Google Play's answer on your phone. As the seller, we see in Google Play Console and its sales and earnings reports what Google gives about each order: its number, the product, the price, tax and currency, when it was made, its state, the model of the device it was bought on, and the buyer's country, with the buyer's state and postcode when Google gives them; and, for a sale where we and not Google are the seller in law (the merchant of record, which depends on the country), the buyer's city too. Google does not show us your name or payment details, though Play Console finds an order by the e-mail address of the Google account that bought it, if you give it to us. Google keeps all of this under its terms.

16. This website

This website, pulse.aldaghir.com, is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no scripts and has no analytics; its fonts and pictures come from the site itself.

When you visit it, GitHub logs your IP address for security purposes, as GitHub says in About GitHub Pages (its part on data collection), under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We do not receive these logs. The app opens these pages in your browser, so the same applies there.

17. Legal basis and your rights

Laws such as the GDPR in the European Union and the United Kingdom require a legal basis for each use of personal data. What never leaves your phone or your server (sections 2 and 3) does not reach us, so we do not process it. For the rest, Aldaghir decides how it is used (the “controller”), on these bases:

  • Push notifications (section 4): to deliver the alerts you set up, the service you asked for (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). Google and Cloudflare handle this data on our behalf.
  • Crash reports (section 5): your consent, which you give by turning the setting on (Article 6(1)(a)). Turning it off withdraws it; reports already sent are deleted within 7 days.
  • The diagnostic data of Google code scanner and of Google's billing library (sections 6 and 15): our legitimate interest in a scanner that needs no camera permission, and in offering Pro through Google Play's billing system, which sends them (Article 6(1)(f)). You can type the code instead of scanning it.
  • Your purchase of Pro (section 15): to sell you what you bought (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • The relay's network data (section 4): our legitimate interest in keeping the relay secure and free of abuse (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Your emails to us: to answer you (Article 6(1)(f)).

This website's logs (section 16) are not ours: GitHub keeps them for its own security and decides how they are used, under its privacy statement. Google, Cloudflare and GitHub run their services in many countries, so the data they handle may be processed outside yours, under their privacy policies.

You may ask us to see the personal data we hold about you, to correct or delete it, to limit or object to its use, and to receive it in a form you can take elsewhere. You may withdraw your consent at any time, and complain to the data protection authority where you live. Write to android@aldaghir.com. With no accounts, this is what we can do:

  • Crash reports carry no name or account, only the phone's maker and model, the versions and the time, so we can tell which are yours only from what you tell us, such as when the app crashed and on which phone. All of them are deleted within 7 days.
  • The push token and the installation identifier are with Google while you have a server: removing your last server deletes them there (section 4). What Google's libraries send is with Google, and the app does not delete it there (sections 4, 6 and 15). Google's privacy policy says how to ask Google.
  • Your order is kept by Google Play. Give us its order number, or the e-mail address of the Google account that bought it, and we can find it in Play Console.
  • This website's logs are GitHub's: ask GitHub.
  • What is on your phone and your server is yours to delete: clear the app's storage or uninstall the app (removing a server leaves a few things, section 3), and uninstall the agent, then delete /var/lib/hlpulse and /etc/hlpulse on your server. What your agent wrote to your server's system log, such as crash reports with this phone's name, stays there until your system removes old logs by its own settings.
  • Your emails: ask, and we delete them.

18. Google Play's Data safety answers

Google Play shows a Data safety summary for the app. These are our answers to its form, one per line, each with what it means; the sections above give the details. They are for the Google Play edition; the GitHub edition does the same, without Pro and Google's billing library.

DS-01 Collects or shares user data
Yes. The push token and installation identifier, with the details of the phone and the app that Firebase sends (section 4), the diagnostic data of the code scanner (section 6) and of Google's billing library (section 15), and crash reports if you turn them on (section 5). None of it is shared.
DS-02 Encrypted in transit
Yes. What the app collects travels over TLS: to your server, from your agent to our relay and on to Google, and from Google's libraries on your phone to Google (sections 4, 6, 11 and 15).
DS-03 Accounts
None. The app has no accounts and no sign-in. Your phone pairs with your own server, which gives it a device token; we keep no account of you.
DS-04 A way to ask for deletion
No. We keep no account to delete: crash reports carry no name and are deleted within 7 days, the push token and the installation identifier are deleted at Google when you remove your last server (section 4), what Google's libraries send is with Google (sections 4, 6 and 15), and your order is kept by Google Play (section 15). You delete what is on your phone and your server yourself (section 17).
DS-05 Device or other IDs
Collected, required, not shared. The push token and the Firebase installation identifier, created at Google once you add a server, to deliver alerts; and the identifiers Google's code scanner adds for its diagnostics. Google handles them for us and keeps them, the push token and the installation identifier until you remove your last server (sections 4 and 6).
DS-06 Crash logs
Collected only if you turn it on, not shared. Off until you turn on crash reports. Scrubbed reports, to find and fix bugs, kept at most 7 days (section 5).
DS-07 Diagnostics
Collected, required, not shared. With no setting to stop them: the details of the phone and the app that Firebase sends when the app registers for push (section 4), and the logs Google's billing library sends about its work when the app connects to Google Play through it to ask about Pro (section 15). Only if you use them: the phone's maker, model and Android version in a crash report you allowed (section 5), and the device, app and performance data Google's code scanner collects; you can type the code instead (section 6).
DS-08 Payment info and purchase history
Not collected. Google Play takes the payment. On your phone, the app reads what your Google account owns and keeps only when Google Play last confirmed Pro; nothing about your purchase is sent to us (section 15).
DS-09 Your server's data, files included
Not collected. Metrics, logs, services, containers, terminal sessions, alerts, settings and, with Pro, the files you download travel only between your phone and your server (section 2).
DS-10 What your alerts say
Not collected. They are encrypted end to end for your phone: our relay and Google pass them on without being able to read them (section 4).
DS-11 Photos and the camera
Not collected. Google's scanner hands the app only the code's text, and the app's own scanner reads the camera on your phone and keeps or sends no picture (section 6).
DS-12 Location, personal details and the rest
Not collected. The app does not collect your location, name, e-mail address, contacts, messages, calendar, health, voice, browsing or what you do in the app, and asks for no location permission.
DS-13 Your phone's name
Not collected. The name you give this phone when pairing goes only to your server.
DS-14 Your server's IP address at our relay
Not kept. Cloudflare handles the connection to deliver each request. The relay logs no addresses; to limit abuse, it counts requests per minute by a hash of the push token or of the sender's address (sections 4 and 5).
DS-15 Independent security review
No. No independent lab has reviewed the app; how it protects your data is on the security page.
DS-16 Children
Not for children. The app is not directed at children (section 10).
