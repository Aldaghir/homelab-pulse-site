Privacy Policy

Effective 10 November 2026

In short

  • No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Your server's data travels only between your phone and your own server.
  • Only two optional things ever reach us: alerts that are end-to-end encrypted so we cannot read them (if you use push notifications), and crash reports (if you turn them on).

1. Who we are

Homelab Pulse (“the app”) is developed by Aldaghir (“we”, “us”). It works together with the Homelab Pulse agent, a program you install on your own server. You can reach us at android@aldaghir.com.

2. Data between your phone and your server

The app talks directly to the agent on your server to show system metrics, storage and network figures, services and containers, logs, alerts, terminal sessions and, with Pro, the files you browse and download, and to apply the settings you choose. This data:

  • travels directly between your phone and your server: on your local network over TLS, with your server's certificate pinned from the pairing code, or remotely through your own Cloudflare Tunnel and Cloudflare Access if you set them up (Cloudflare then handles that traffic under your Cloudflare account and Cloudflare's privacy policy);
  • is never sent to us.

3. Data stored on your phone

  • The servers you add: name, address, certificate fingerprint and the agent's features.
  • Access tokens for your servers, the end of your access if it is a temporary guest access, Cloudflare Access credentials if you use them, and the private key that decrypts your push notifications, encrypted with a key kept by the Android Keystore.
  • If you remove a server while it cannot be reached, what the app needs to tell it later that this phone is unpaired, and nothing else: its address at home and certificate fingerprint, this phone's device identifier and access token there, and, if you use them, its tunnel address and Cloudflare Access credentials. They are encrypted like the other secrets, in a file of their own that no exported backup holds or changes. Android's task scheduler keeps the server's identifier and when to try next. The app tries in the background whenever the phone has a network, waiting longer each time, and deletes what it kept once the server confirms the unpairing, or after 7 days at the latest, told or not. The task scheduler then keeps a short record of these tries, the server's identifier among it, until it clears it by itself a day or more later.
  • The latest data the app showed you from your servers, so it can show it with its age when there is no connection: readings, server details, storage (disks, their mount points and health, Docker images), containers and services, alerts and their rules, the state of backups, updates, security, network and Uptime Kuma, checks (without the credentials in their targets), the timeline (including SSH sign-ins with their user names and addresses), devices and the audit log (including the address each action came from). It stays until newer data replaces it, you remove or re-pair the server, your temporary access ends, or the server refuses this phone.
  • On a server where you are a viewer, which alerts you removed from your list (their identifiers and when you removed them), so that they stay off this phone and its notifications. Nothing goes to the server, and they go when the server no longer lists those alerts, you remove or re-pair the server, your temporary access ends, or the server refuses this phone.
  • With Pro: the files you download, in the folder you choose; they are yours and stay there. While a download is running or paused, the app keeps what it needs to go on: the server, the paths and names of the files and folders and the files' sizes, the folder you chose, a short-lived ticket from your server, and how far it got (the files and folders made so far, how much of each file is saved, why it paused, and the version tag your server gave each file, made from its size, its place on the disk and when it changed). This goes when the download ends, you cancel it, or you remove the server, and the app then normally gives up its permission to read and write in the folder you chose, unless another download still uses it. For one file, that permission is to the file itself and stays once the file is downloaded, so that its notification can open it, until Android drops the oldest ones past its limit. If you choose where to save a file while the app is not yet connected to your server (Android may hold the network back from the app while you choose), the download waits for the connection before it starts: meanwhile the app keeps the place and name of the file you chose, with Android's task scheduler too, so that this file, still empty, is deleted if the download never starts (you cancel it, leave the file's screen, or the app is closed first); the scheduler keeps that short record until it clears it by itself, a day or more later. Android's task scheduler keeps a short record of a download that ended or was cancelled until it clears it by itself, a day or more later, even if you remove the server: for one file, the server, the file's path, name, size and version tag, the folder, why it failed if it did, and the ticket, which expires within a day; for several files, the server, the download's name (the folder's, or that of the first item you chose), the number of files and their total size, how many were downloaded or failed and how much was downloaded, and when it ended and why. While a download runs, its notification shows its name and progress; the notification that tells how it ended stays until you dismiss it, or until you remove the server while Android still keeps the download's record.
  • If you buy Pro on Google Play: when Google Play last confirmed the purchase, sealed with a key kept by the Android Keystore. Google Play handles the payment under Google's privacy policy; the app keeps no payment details.
  • Your settings in the app, including the quick commands you write for the terminal.

This data is excluded from Android cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. If you export a backup, the file holds your servers, their access tokens and other secrets, and your settings, encrypted with a password you choose, and stays wherever you save it. Removing a server, or uninstalling the app, deletes the related data from your phone, except that removing a server leaves the task scheduler's short record of its downloads, which Android clears by itself a day or more later, and, if the server could not be reached, what is needed to unpair this phone from it, for 7 days at most, and the task scheduler's short record of those tries, which Android clears by itself a day or more later (above); files you downloaded stay in the folder you chose.

4. Push notifications (optional)

If your phone has Google Play services and you add a server, the app registers with Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM), a Google service, to receive alerts. Registering creates a push token and a Firebase installation identifier at Google. It does not happen before you add your first server, and the registration is deleted when you remove your last server.

The app gives the push token to your agent. When an alert fires, your agent encrypts it for your phone (X25519 and AES-256-GCM) and sends the sealed message, with the push token, to Pulse Relay, a small service we run on Cloudflare Workers that hands it to FCM. The relay cannot read your alerts, stores nothing, and does not log tokens, addresses or message contents. Cloudflare, as our hosting provider, handles the network connection (including your server's IP address) to deliver each request, under Cloudflare's privacy policy. FCM then delivers the message under Google's privacy policy. You can run your own relay instead, or rely on Telegram alerts from your agent and not use push at all.

5. Crash reports (optional, off by default)

If you turn on “Send crash reports” in Settings, the app keeps a report when it crashes and sends it the next time it starts, through your own agent. A report contains the app and Android versions, the phone model, the types of the errors and where in the code they happened, and the error messages with addresses, host names, e-mail addresses and tokens removed.

Your agent writes the report to your server's system log and, when a relay is configured, forwards it to us. We use crash reports only to find and fix bugs. They stay in our relay's log on Cloudflare for at most 7 days and are then deleted automatically. Nothing is recorded while the setting is off, and turning it off deletes any report still waiting on your phone.

6. Scanning a pairing code

The app reads pairing QR codes with Google code scanner, part of Google Play services. Google Play services handles the camera, the scan runs on your phone, and the app receives only the text of the code. Google may collect diagnostic data about the scanner's use, such as the device model, app version and performance figures, as described in Google's ML Kit data disclosure.

When Google code scanner is missing or cannot open on your phone, the app scans with its own scanner instead. It asks for the camera permission the first time, reads the camera's picture on your phone only while the scanner is open, and keeps or sends no picture: only the text of the code is used. You can type the code instead of scanning it.

7. Services you connect

Integrations you set up, such as Telegram, Cloudflare or Uptime Kuma, are run by your agent. Credentials you enter in the app go directly to your agent, which stores them encrypted on your server; they never reach us. Those services' own terms and privacy policies apply to them.

8. Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to reach your server and choose between your local network and remote access.
  • Wi-Fi multicast: to find agents on your local network.
  • Notifications: to show alerts, and with Pro a download's progress and how it ended; asked for when needed.
  • Camera: only for the app's own QR scanner, when Google code scanner cannot open; asked for the first time. The picture is read on your phone and is not kept or sent.
  • Biometrics: for the optional app lock. Android checks your fingerprint or face; the app never receives biometric data.
  • Run at startup and wake lock: added by Android libraries to deliver notifications and refresh widgets, and with Pro to resume a download after the phone restarts.
  • Foreground service (data sync): with Pro, to keep a download transferring while the app is in the background, its progress shown in a notification.

9. What we never do

We have no user accounts, show no ads and include no analytics or tracking libraries. We do not sell or share personal data, and we do not build profiles.

10. Children

The app is a tool for managing servers and is not directed at children under 13, or under the minimum age in your country.

11. Security

Every connection is encrypted with TLS, and your server's certificate is pinned on your phone. Secrets on the phone are encrypted with Android Keystore keys. The agent stores only hashes of device tokens and enforces a permission level for each device. No system is perfectly secure; if you find a vulnerability, please tell us at android@aldaghir.com.

12. Your choices

  • Turn crash reports on or off at any time in Settings.
  • Turn notifications off in Android settings. Removing a server unpairs your phone from it, so it stops sending you alerts. If the server cannot be reached then, the app says so and unpairs your phone once it can, within 7 days; alerts the server sends meanwhile are dropped on your phone, never shown.
  • We keep no account or profile about you. The only data we hold are crash reports, which are deleted within 7 days. You are still welcome to contact us with any request.

13. Changes

We will post any change on this page with a new effective date, and mention important changes in the app's release notes.
