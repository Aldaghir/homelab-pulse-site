For Android · first release coming soon to Google Play

Your server stays up all night. You don’t have to.

Homelab Pulse is an Android app and a small agent you install on your home server. The agent watches the server around the clock and wakes your phone only when something needs you. The app shows you everything, and lets you act from anywhere without opening a single port.

What happened while you slept, line by line.

One night on a home server, told with the app’s own screens.

  1. 02:39CRIT

    Container immich-server is unhealthy

    Your phone is asleep. The server isn’t.

    The agent checks your alert rules on the server itself, so nothing waits for your phone to wake up. The alert reaches it as a push only your phone can read: encrypted end to end, with a key made when you paired.

  2. 02:44CRIT

    A critical alert left unacknowledged for five minutes goes to Telegram as well, through a bot you create and own. Your server sends it to Telegram directly, so it reaches a phone without Google services too.

  3. 07:30INFO

    home-srv up 1 day 2 hours · containers 5/6 healthy

    One look with your morning coffee.

  4. 12:10OK

    Fixed from the office, with nothing opened at home.

    Away from home the app reaches your server through Cloudflare Tunnel, behind Cloudflare Access, and switches between home and away by itself. Restarting asks for your fingerprint or screen lock first, and the server writes it in the audit log.

02 What it watches

One small agent. Your whole homelab.

Health
CPU and each of its cores, memory, temperature, storage with disk health, and network, live every two seconds. The history stays on your server: up to 30 days of charts in the app.
Containers and services
Docker containers and systemd services with their status, health, CPU and memory. Start, stop and restart them, and read their logs live, filtered by level.
Alerts
Seventeen built-in rules, sixteen of them on from the start, and HTTP and TCP checks of your own, all checked on the server. They reach you as an encrypted push, on Telegram, or both.
Terminal
A real terminal on your server, where htop and vim work and Arabic shows as it should. If the connection drops, the session waits up to 30 minutes for you.
Homelab
Backup freshness (by default, a backup older than 26 hours counts as failed), pending updates, fail2ban and UFW, and your Uptime Kuma monitors next to your server’s own state.
Family and team
As many servers as you like. Invite another phone with a one-time code and a role, end its access at once, and see every change it made in the audit log.

03 How it works

A small agent, your phone, and no account with us.

The agent is one program on your server. It keeps the history, checks the rules and sends the alerts. Your phone talks to it directly at home, and through Cloudflare when you’re away.

  • At home Straight to the agent over TLS, on port 8443 by default. The app trusts only the certificate in the QR code you scanned.
  • Away from home Through Cloudflare Tunnel, which your server opens outward, behind Cloudflare Access. Cloudflare can see this traffic at its edge, as the privacy policy says.
  • Alerts Encrypted on the server for your phone, then passed by the Pulse relay to Firebase Cloud Messaging, neither of which can read them. Or sent by your own Telegram bot.

04 Security

What keeps your server yours.

The app can restart services and open a shell on your server, so each step is guarded.

The security model, and how to report a problem

Read the privacy policy

  1. SHA-256

    A pinned certificate

    The QR code carries the agent’s certificate fingerprint. On your home network the app trusts that certificate and no other, and refuses to connect if it changes.

  2. 256-bit

    A token for each phone

    Each phone gets its own token, which the agent keeps only as a hash. A pairing code works once, within ten minutes.

  3. viewer · operator · admin

    A role for each phone

    Choose what each phone may do. Ending a phone’s access cuts its live data and its terminal at once.

  4. Android Keystore

    Your fingerprint for risky actions

    The terminal, stopping or restarting, rebooting, rules, integrations, invites and remote access need a fresh signature from a key in your phone’s Keystore, unlocked by your fingerprint or screen lock. Without it, the server refuses.

  5. AES-256-GCM

    Keys nobody reads back

    Your Telegram, Cloudflare and Uptime Kuma keys are stored encrypted on your server. The app can replace them but never read them, and no log shows them.

  6. no sudo

    The least it needs

    The agent runs without sudo. A small helper runs the few commands that need root, and only in the fixed forms its configuration allows.
